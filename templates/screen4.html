Section 4.1 § Methodological Formulation

4.2 Adaptive Token Verification & Confidence Boundary Pruning

In conventional speculative decoding paradigms, an auxiliary small-scale draft model $\mathcal{M}_d$ generates a speculative sequence of $K$ candidate tokens $\{\tilde{x}_{t+1}, \dots, \tilde{x}_{t+K}\}$, which is subsequently verified in a single forward pass by the primary autoregressive target model $\mathcal{M}_t$.

Marginal Note · Dr. VanceMethodology §4

“Key claim: Reaches 3.4x peak acceleration without incurring memory pressure from an auxiliary draft network.”

Rather than maintaining a persistent draft network—which incurs severe KV-cache memory pressure and latency overhead on edge GPUs—we formulate an internal trajectory verification operator $\mathcal{T}_{\tau}$. Let the target model conditional likelihood be defined as:

$$\hat{y}_t = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{V}} P(x_t \mid x_{<t}; \Theta_{\text{target}})$$
(Eq. 4 · Joint Acceptance Kernel)

Under condition $\tau \in [0.82, 0.95]$, candidate rejection cascades are pruned prior to self-attention layer 18, circumventing uninformative token compute. The acceptance probability $\alpha_k$ for candidate draft token $\tilde{x}_{t+k}$ at index position $k \le K$ is evaluated simultaneously using the normalized density ratio:

$$\alpha_k = \min\left(1, \frac{q(\tilde{x}_{t+k} \mid x_{<t+k})}{p(\tilde{x}_{t+k} \mid x_{<t+k})}\right)$$
(Eq. 5 · Boundary Density Ratio)

Crucially, our dynamic boundary criterion guarantees that the marginal output distribution precisely recovers the unmodified autoregressive distribution of $\mathcal{M}_t$, eliminating any risk of qualitative drift or degradation in open-domain reasoning tasks.
